Lore of Aden

Chapter I

The Shattered Sphere

In the beginning there was no Aden and no Elmore, no Humans, Elves, Dwarves or Orcs. All that would ever exist lay together inside one sphere. Within it, two powers slowly became aware of themselves: a white light that took the form of a woman and named herself Einhasad, and a darkness that took the form of a man and named himself Gran Kain.

Together they broke free of the sphere, and in breaking it they made the world. The shards that fell became the ground, the shards that rose became the sky, and the spirits that had slept inside became every beast and every growing thing.

The greatest and wisest of those spirits were the Giants. They looked up to Einhasad and Gran Kain as parents and swore to keep faith with them forever, and the two gods, pleased, made them lords over all living creatures.

Chapter II

Children of the Gods

Einhasad and Gran Kain also had children of their own. The first five were Shilen, goddess of water; Paagrio, god of fire; Maphr, goddess of earth; Sayha, god of wind; and Eva, whose songs accompanied the other gods as they worked. Each shaped a people in the image of their element:

  • The Elves, born of Shilen's water, wise and gifted in magic, served the Giants in politics and sorcery.
  • The Orcs, into whom Paagrio breathed fire, gained endless strength and will and became the Giants' warriors.
  • The Dwarves, shaped by Maphr, could build and calculate like no one else and ran the Giants' industry and trade.
  • The Arteias, children of Sayha's wind, wandered the world bringing news back to the Giants, until they vanished from it.

Humans came last, and not from love. Gran Kain gathered what his children had thrown away (stagnant water, dying fire, barren earth and wild wind) and pieced together the first people. Weak and despised, they were handed to the Giants as servants. Yet of all the races, it was the one made from leftovers that would one day rule the continent.

Since humans are made of the refuse of the gods, they are naturally restless. Give them no enemy and they will make one of their neighbour.

Chapter III

The Fall of the Giants

Over the ages the Giants built a civilization so advanced that it began to rival the gods. Their capital, Grandferion, was a city of wonders, and their scholars learned to bend nature, forge impossible weapons and, at last, to create life itself: the one power Einhasad kept for herself alone.

In their laboratories they made the Kamael, a race of living weapons that divine power could barely touch. To Einhasad this was rebellion. She sent her angels against the Giants, and a war broke out that shook heaven and earth.

When the Giants would not yield, Einhasad borrowed Gran Kain's Star Hammer, the Hammer of Despair, a weapon even its owner feared to use. It fell on Grandferion. Stars rained from the sky, cities vanished and the greatest civilization the world had known was gone in a single day. The survivors were hunted across the continent, and the Kamael were sealed away on the distant Isle of Souls.

Chapter IV

Shilen's Betrayal

Shilen did not stand with her mother. For her part in the age of the Giants she was cast down: her waters were given to her sister Eva, and Shilen became the goddess of death, ruling from a realm at the bottom of the abyss.

Her Elves were torn apart. Those who stayed loyal to the light followed Eva and remained the Elves of the forests. Those who followed their fallen mother into the dark became the Dark Elves, masters of forbidden magic, the Children of Shilen.

From the union of Shilen and Gran Kain came the great dragons, mightiest among them Antharas of the earth and Valakas of fire. When Shilen was defeated they went to ground: Antharas in the lair beneath Dragon Valley near Giran, Valakas in the flames of the Forge of the Gods. There they sleep and wait for her return. Shilen left behind her most beloved daughter, Lilith, cursed with endless life, to prepare the way.

Chapter V

The Empire of Elmoreden

With the Giants gone, Elves, Orcs and Humans fought for the continent for generations. Humans, dying in their thousands, prayed for a god who would promise them something after death, and many turned to Shilen. When the wars finally burned out, it was Humankind that stood on the ashes.

The emperor Shunaiman united the lands of Aden and Elmore into the Empire of Elmoreden. At his crowning a winged girl wreathed in holy fire descended from the sky: Anakim, envoy of Einhasad. Shunaiman declared Einhasad the only true faith, struck Gran Kain from the story of humanity's creation, and outlawed the worship of darkness.

The Children of Shilen refused to kneel. Under Lilith they raised the Necropolis and the Catacombs and an elite army, the Lilim. Against them Anakim led the Nephilim, giant armored warriors of light. Their war never truly ended: it still echoes in the Seven Signs, where the Lords of Dawn and the Revolutionaries of Dusk fight over the seals.

Chapter VI

The Tower of Insolence

Generations later, Emperor Baium sat on the throne of Elmoreden and wanted more than an empire. Whispered on by dark powers, he ordered a tower raised high enough to reach the gods themselves: the Tower of Insolence.

The gods answered his arrogance by sealing him inside it, frozen for eternity at the top of his own monument, where he waits for anyone foolish enough to wake him. His heirs, his daughter Solina and his son Frintezza, fought over what was left. The empire split in two, into the Kingdom of Aden in the south and the Kingdom of Elmore in the north, and within a few decades Elmoreden was only a memory. Frintezza's restless spirit still haunts the Imperial Tomb.

Chapter VII

The Chaotic Era

The kings of Aden kept their crowns but lost their grip on the land. The turning point came at the Battle for Giran Castle, where an adventurer of common birth took a castle and, by the old rules of siege, became its rightful lord. From that day on, anyone with enough sword-arms behind them could rule.

Adventurers bound by friendship or blood formed clans, clans formed alliances, and the seven castles of Aden (Gludio, Dion, Giran, Oren, Aden, Innadril and Goddard, later joined by Rune and Schuttgart) changed hands in endless sieges. The bravest learned the secrets of the Giants, rose as Noblesse and fought in the Grand Olympiad for the right to be called Hero.

Chapter VIII

Beyond the Sea

The past would not stay buried. The Kamael, the Giants' living weapons, returned from the Isle of Souls, called by Eva to defend Aden rather than destroy it. But tipping the balance toward the light came at a price.

On the cursed island of Hellbound, demons ruled over enslaved natives until adventurers broke their hold. Across the sea, the forgotten continent of Gracia was found again, and with it the Seeds: monstrous growths spreading from the land. Aden's alliance built its base at Keucereus and sent its strongest into the Seed of Infinity, where the undying Ekimus waits, and the Seed of Destruction, where the commander Tiat gathers his army.

Chapter IX

The Ice Queen and High Five

In the far north beyond Schuttgart, Freya, the Ice Queen, woke in her frozen castle and spread an endless winter across the land. In Dragon Valley the earth trembles again where Antharas sleeps, and the fires around Valakas burn hotter every season.

This is the age of High Five, where the story of Aden stands today. The gods are silent, the dragons are stirring, and every castle, every raid and every Hero's title is still there to be won. The rest of the story is yours to write.
